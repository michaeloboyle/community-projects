# Open Source Project Intake: Rules and Processes


Open Source Project Intake
Rules, Eligibility, and Review Process

Purpose
The Agentics Foundation exists to support, coordinate, and steward high-quality open source projects that advance the agentic AI ecosystem.
This intake process provides a controlled, member-only mechanism for submitting open source projects for review, support, collaboration, or inclusion in Foundation activities.
The process is intentionally simple, while preserving governance integrity and community trust.

Eligibility & Access Rules
Member-Only Access
Only registered members of the Agentics Foundation may submit projects through the intake process.
Submissions from non-members will not be reviewed.
Membership must be active and in good standing at the time of submission and review.
Project Requirements
To be eligible for review, a project must:
Be an open source project hosted in a public repository (e.g. GitHub)
Clearly state its license
Align with the Foundation’s mission, values, and code of conduct
Not promote illegal activity, malicious software, or deceptive practices
Not infringe on known intellectual property rights
The Foundation reserves the right to decline review of projects that fail to meet these criteria.

Eligible Submission Categories
Members may submit an open source project for one or more of the following purposes:
Project Donation to the Agentics Foundation Request to transfer long-term stewardship of the project to the Foundation.
Foundation Website Listing Request to have the project featured on the Agentics Foundation website as a community project.
Co-Founder Search Request to connect with potential co-founders from within the Foundation membership.
Problem Support Request Request help from Foundation members to overcome a defined technical, architectural, or organizational challenge.
Contributor Engagement Request to attract contributors, reviewers, or collaborators from the Foundation community.

Submission Process
Step 1: Online Intake Form
Members submit projects via a form on the Agentics Foundation website and must provide:
Full name
Email address
LinkedIn profile link
GitHub profile link
GitHub repository link
Submission category (dropdown selection)
A brief description (maximum 500 characters) clearly describing:
The project
The specific request being made to the Foundation
Incomplete or non-compliant submissions may be declined without review.

Review Authority
All submissions are reviewed by the Open Source Committee, a standing committee of the Agentics Foundation.
The committee reviews submissions during scheduled meetings and evaluates each request independently.

Review & Voting Process
Each submission undergoes a two-step voting process.

Vote 1: Escalation Determination
Question:
Does this submission require escalation to senior leadership of the Foundation?
A simple majority (50% + 1) triggers escalation.
Escalation is most commonly expected for:
Project donation requests
Requests involving governance, legal, or intellectual property considerations
Projects that may become core Foundation assets
If escalation passes, the submission is referred to designated senior leaders for further review and decision.

Vote 2: Validation Decision (If Not Escalated)
If escalation is not required, the committee votes on validation.
Question:
Should this request be approved and supported by the Foundation?
Possible outcomes:
Approved – The Foundation proceeds with the requested action
Declined – The request is rejected
Deferred – Additional clarification or information is required
A simple majority determines the outcome.

Retraction & Removal of Previously Approved Projects
The Agentics Foundation reserves the right to retract approval of any previously approved project, regardless of category.
Grounds for Retraction May Include:
Violation of the Foundation’s code of conduct or values
Misrepresentation of the project or its licensing
Inactive or abandoned maintenance that impacts community trust
Legal, ethical, or reputational risk to the Foundation
Loss of membership standing by the submitting member
Retraction Process
Any committee member may propose a retraction for review.
The Open Source Committee votes on retraction during a scheduled meeting.
A simple majority (50% + 1) is required to approve retraction.
For high-risk cases, the committee may escalate the retraction decision to senior leadership.
Retraction may result in:
Removal from the Foundation website
Withdrawal of Foundation support or promotion
Termination of any active collaboration facilitated by the Foundation

Communication of Decisions
Submitters will be notified of decisions via email.
Approved submissions will receive guidance on next steps and engagement.
Retractions and removals will be communicated to the submitter with appropriate context.

Scope & Disclaimers
Submission does not guarantee approval, endorsement, or long-term support.
The Foundation does not assume ownership, liability, or responsibility for projects unless explicitly agreed through a separate process.
All decisions are made in the interest of the Foundation and its membership.

Guiding Principles
This process is designed to be:
Member-focused
Transparent
Low-friction
Governance-aware
Scalable with the community









Internal Scoring Rubric (Committee Use)

Overview
This rubric is used by the Open Source Committee to:
Evaluate submitted projects consistently
Inform validation, escalation, or retraction decisions
Maintain quality and alignment across Foundation-supported initiatives
The rubric is advisory, not mechanical. Scores guide discussion but do not replace judgment.

Scoring Method
Each criterion is scored on a 0–5 scale.
0 – Does not meet minimum expectations
1 – Very weak alignment or quality
2 – Below average, material gaps present
3 – Acceptable / baseline
4 – Strong
5 – Exceptional
Each submission is scored across all criteria. Total possible score: 25 points.

Evaluation Criteria
1. Mission & Values Alignment (0–5)
Assesses: How well the project aligns with the Agentics Foundation’s mission, principles, and ethical standards.
Consider:
Relevance to agentic AI, infrastructure, governance, or ecosystem enablement
Alignment with open source values
Absence of harmful, deceptive, or exploitative intent
Guidance:
0–1: Misaligned or unclear purpose
3: Generally aligned but not core
5: Strongly reinforces Foundation mission

2. Project Quality & Maturity (0–5)
Assesses: The technical and organizational quality of the project.
Consider:
Repository structure and documentation
License clarity
Evidence of working code or active development
Issue tracking and basic hygiene
Guidance:
0–1: Incomplete, poorly documented, or unclear
3: Functional and understandable
5: Well-structured, production-ready, or exemplary

3. Clarity of Request (0–5)
Assesses: How clearly the submitter articulated what they want from the Foundation.
Consider:
Specificity of the 500-character description
Feasibility of the request
Alignment between category selected and description
Guidance:
0–1: Vague or confusing
3: Clear but limited detail
5: Precise, focused, and actionable

4. Community Impact & Engagement Potential (0–5)
Assesses: The potential value of the project to the Foundation membership and broader community.
Consider:
Opportunity for collaboration or learning
Relevance to multiple members
Likelihood of attracting contributors or engagement
Guidance:
0–1: Narrow or self-serving
3: Useful to a subset of members
5: Broad, high-impact community value

5. Risk & Governance Considerations (0–5)
Assesses: Potential legal, ethical, operational, or reputational risk.
Consider:
IP or licensing ambiguity
Security or safety concerns
Dependency risks
Governance complexity
Scoring is inverted (lower risk = higher score):
0–1: High or unclear risk
3: Manageable risk
5: Minimal or no apparent risk

Score Interpretation Guidelines
Total Score | Suggested Outcome
21–25 | Strong candidate for approval or escalation
16–20 | Approve or approve with conditions
11–15 | Defer or request clarification
0–10 | Decline
These ranges are guidance only. The committee may override outcomes with rationale.

Escalation Signal
Independent of total score, automatic escalation consideration should occur if:
The project is offered for donation to the Foundation
Legal, licensing, or IP concerns are identified
The project may become a core Foundation asset
Reputational risk exists despite high overall score

Use in Retraction Reviews
The same rubric may be reused during retraction evaluations, with emphasis on:
Risk & Governance Considerations
Mission & Values Alignment
Ongoing Project Quality & Maintenance
Significant score degradation over time may justify retraction.

Record Keeping
Scores should be captured in committee notes or internal tooling.
Individual scores need not be disclosed externally.
Aggregate scoring trends may be reviewed periodically to improve intake quality.

Edge Cases & Special Situations
(Governance Guardrails)
These situations require extra scrutiny, regardless of score.

A. Conflict of Interest
Scenario: A committee member is:
A contributor
A maintainer
A co-founder
Financially or professionally involved
Rule:
Member must declare conflict
Member must abstain from voting
Abstention is recorded but does not block quorum

B. Competing or Overlapping Projects
Scenario: Two submitted projects overlap in scope or goals.
Guidance:
Committee does not choose winners
Multiple projects may be approved
Avoid language implying endorsement superiority
Encourage collaboration where appropriate

C. Commercially Adjacent Projects
Scenario: Open source core with commercial extensions or services.
Guidance:
Allowed if OSS license is clear
Foundation does not promote paid offerings
Website listings must clearly label scope

D. Abandoned or Inactive Projects
Scenario: Previously approved project becomes dormant.
Guidance:
Inactivity alone ≠ retraction
Inactivity + community confusion or risk → retraction review
Committee may request maintainer status update before action

E. Behavioral or Conduct Issues
Scenario: Maintainer violates Foundation code of conduct.
Guidance:
Conduct issues override technical merit
Retraction may proceed even if project is strong
Escalate if reputational risk is high

Open Source Committee Charter

1. Establishment
The Open Source Committee (“Committee”) is a standing committee of the Agentics Foundation (“Foundation”), established by the Board of Directors to oversee the intake, review, governance, and lifecycle management of open source projects submitted by Foundation members.

2. Purpose & Mandate
The Committee is responsible for:
Reviewing member-submitted open source projects
Evaluating requests for Foundation support, visibility, collaboration, or stewardship
Recommending escalation of submissions requiring senior leadership or Board review
Maintaining quality, integrity, and alignment across Foundation-supported projects
Recommending retraction or removal of previously approved projects when warranted
The Committee acts as a governance and signal-filtering body, not as an execution or development team.

3. Scope of Authority
The Committee has authority to:
Approve, decline, defer, or recommend support for submissions within its mandate
Maintain and apply scoring rubrics and review frameworks
Recommend project listings, contributor engagement, or community support
Initiate retraction reviews of previously approved projects
Escalate submissions or retraction decisions to senior leadership when appropriate
The Committee does not:
Assume ownership or liability for projects
Bind the Foundation to legal, financial, or IP obligations
Override decisions reserved for the Board or senior leadership

4. Membership & Composition
Committee members are appointed by the Board or its delegate.
Members must be active Foundation members in good standing.
The Committee may include technical, governance, and community representatives.
A Chair may be designated to facilitate meetings and agendas.

5. Conflicts of Interest
Members must disclose any actual or perceived conflicts of interest.
Members with conflicts must recuse themselves from discussion and voting.
Recusals are recorded but do not affect quorum.

6. Meetings & Quorum
The Committee meets on a regular cadence as determined internally.
A quorum consists of a simple majority of active committee members.
Meetings may be conducted virtually.

7. Voting & Decision-Making
All formal decisions require a simple majority (50% + 1) of voting members present.
The Committee conducts two primary votes for submissions:
Escalation Vote Determines whether a submission requires senior leadership review.
Validation Vote Determines approval, deferral, or rejection when escalation is not required.

8. Retraction Authority
The Committee may initiate a retraction review for any previously approved project.
Grounds include, but are not limited to:
Violation of Foundation values or code of conduct
Licensing or IP changes introducing risk
Misrepresentation of project status or intent
Reputational, legal, or governance risk
Loss of membership standing by the submitter
Retraction decisions require a simple majority and may be escalated when risk is significant.

9. Records & Transparency
Decisions are documented internally.
Scores and deliberations are confidential unless otherwise determined.
Submitters are notified of outcomes and next steps.

10. Amendments
This Charter may be amended by the Board of Directors or its authorized delegate.




Bylaws Language: Open Source Governance
(This section is written to be inserted directly into the Foundation bylaws.)

Article X — Open Source Project Governance

Section 10.1 — Open Source Committee
The Foundation shall maintain a standing Open Source Committee responsible for the intake, review, and governance oversight of open source projects submitted by Foundation members.
The Committee operates under authority delegated by the Board of Directors and in accordance with Foundation policies and values.

Section 10.2 — Eligibility for Submission
Only registered members of the Foundation in good standing may submit open source projects for review.
Projects must:
Be publicly accessible
Declare an open source license
Align with the Foundation’s mission and code of conduct
The Foundation reserves the right to decline review of non-compliant submissions.

Section 10.3 — Submission Categories
Members may submit projects for purposes including, but not limited to:
Requesting Foundation stewardship or donation
Seeking visibility on Foundation platforms
Requesting co-founders or contributors
Requesting community or technical support
Submission does not guarantee approval or endorsement.

Section 10.4 — Review & Escalation
The Open Source Committee shall review submissions using internally defined criteria.
The Committee may:
Approve, decline, or defer submissions
Escalate submissions to senior leadership or the Board when governance, legal, or reputational considerations exist
Escalation requires approval by a simple majority of the Committee.

Section 10.5 — Retraction & Removal
The Foundation retains the right to withdraw approval or support for any project previously approved.
Grounds for retraction include:
Violation of Foundation policies or values
Licensing or governance changes
Risk to the Foundation or its members
Conduct issues involving project maintainers
Retraction decisions may be made by the Committee or escalated to senior leadership or the Board as appropriate.

Section 10.6 — Limitation of Responsibility
Approval or listing of a project does not constitute:
Ownership
Endorsement
Assumption of liability
Legal or financial obligation
Such obligations may only be assumed through separate, explicit agreements.

Section 10.7 — Governance Flexibility
The Board of Directors may modify, delegate, or revoke authority granted under this Article as necessary to protect the interests of the Foundation.
